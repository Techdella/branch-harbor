Every location wants its own page. Customers search for "near me", compare opening hours and look at photos of the actual place they'll visit. A single national page can't answer those questions — but fifty independently built local sites usually answer them badly.

Brand governance is the set of rules and tools that let local pages exist without drifting away from the brand. Done well, it's mostly invisible: locations edit what they're allowed to edit, and everything else simply stays correct.

## The three kinds of content

Sort every piece of content on a location page into one of three groups.

**Locked content** belongs to the brand and is the same everywhere: logo, colours, typography, navigation, legal footer, privacy notice, accessibility statement, core service descriptions. Locations can see it but not change it.

**Editable content** belongs to the location: address, phone, opening hours, team members, local photos, timetable or menu availability, local news. Locations change it whenever they need to, without asking.

**Approval content** sits in between: anything that creates risk if it's wrong. Prices outside an agreed range, promotional claims, health or results statements, new pages, and changes to regulated wording. Locations can propose a change; someone at headquarters or regional level approves it.

Write this classification down as a table and keep it next to your brand guidelines. It's more useful to a franchisee than a 60-page brand book.

## Govern fields, not pages

A common mistake is to approve whole pages. That makes every typo fix a ticket. Instead, attach the rule to the field: the hours field is editable, the hero headline is locked, the offer price is editable within a range, the offer terms need approval. Locations then move quickly where the risk is low, and reviews focus on the few fields that matter.

## Publish as a group, roll back as a group

When headquarters changes something shared — a new logo, a revised legal notice, an updated service description — it should go to every location page at once, with a preview of what changes where. Two safeguards matter:

- **Local overrides are visible.** If a location has edited a field that headquarters is now changing, the publisher should see that conflict before publishing, not after.
- **Every publish can be rolled back.** Mistakes happen. A group publish that partially fails, or publishes the wrong image, should be reversible in one step, with a record of what happened.

## Keep the facts in one place

The most damaging inconsistencies are factual: old opening hours on the website, different hours on the map listing, and a third version on a social profile. Keep each location's core facts — name, address, phone, hours, services — in one record, and publish them from there to the website and structured data. When the hours change, they change everywhere.

## Accessibility and language are brand rules too

A brand that is consistent but unusable isn't consistent with anything worth protecting. Build accessibility into the locked layout — contrast, heading order, alt text prompts, keyboard navigation — so locations can't accidentally break it. If you operate in several languages, make language versions part of the template rather than separate sites, so a translated page inherits the same governance.

## Signs your governance is working

- Locations update hours and photos themselves, without asking headquarters.
- Approval queues are short and mostly about offers and claims.
- Search results show the same facts as the website.
- A brand refresh reaches every location in a day, not a quarter.
- Nobody keeps a private copy of the logo "just in case".
