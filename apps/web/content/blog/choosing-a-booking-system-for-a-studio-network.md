A single studio can choose booking software on a demo and a gut feeling. A network can't. Once five or fifty studios depend on a system for schedules, memberships, payments and customer records, changing it later is slow and disruptive. This guide lists the questions worth asking before you commit.

## 1. How does it model locations?

Some systems were built for one studio and added "multi-location" later. Check:

- Can each studio have its own schedule, rooms, instructors, prices and time zone?
- Can a head office see every studio, while a studio manager sees only their own?
- In a franchise, can each franchisee see their own studios and nothing else?
- Can you add a studio in a new country without a separate account?

## 2. Can memberships work across studios?

Members increasingly expect to use one membership at several studios. Decide your policy first, then test whether the system supports it:

- **Home studio only**, with drop-in rates elsewhere.
- **Network-wide membership**, valid at every studio.
- **Regional passes**, valid at a group of studios.

If studios are independently owned, ask how revenue from a member's visit to another studio is shared, and whether the system can report it.

## 3. Who owns the payments?

In a franchise, each franchisee usually needs to be the merchant of record for their own sales, with their own payout account. Ask whether the system supports separate payment accounts per studio or per owner, which payment providers are available in each of your countries, and how refunds and disputes are handled.

## 4. What about classes, waitlists and cover?

Core studio workflows vary more than they seem. Look at:

- Waitlists that promote automatically, and what happens when a promoted member doesn't confirm.
- Late-cancellation and no-show policies, per studio.
- Instructor cover requests and how substitutions appear to members.
- Class packs and credits — and whether they can be used across studios.
- Intro offers and trials, with limits so they're used once per person.

## 5. Can you get your data out?

Ask for a sample export before you sign. You should be able to export members, memberships, visits, payments and consent records in a usable format, at any time, without a fee. Check whether there is an API, what it costs, and whether it covers the data you need for reporting and marketing.

## 6. Does it fit with your website and marketing?

Your website needs live schedules and booking per studio. Your marketing team needs to know where members came from. Check how schedules appear on your site (embedded widget, API or plugin), whether enquiries and intro offers can be tracked per studio, and whether consent to marketing is recorded with its source.

## 7. How does it handle languages and currencies?

If you operate — or plan to operate — in more than one country, check the languages available to staff and members, local date and time formats, tax handling, and whether reports keep currencies separate rather than adding them together.

## A simple scoring sheet

Score each candidate from 0 to 3 on each question, weighted for what matters to your network. A typical weighting for a franchise network:

| Area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Location and permission model | 3 |
| Payments per owner | 3 |
| Data export and API | 3 |
| Cross-studio memberships | 2 |
| Class workflows | 2 |
| Website and marketing fit | 2 |
| Languages and currencies | 1–3, depending on your plans |

Ask two existing customers with a similar number of locations what migration and support were really like.
