> **Not legal advice.** This article is general information to help you prepare questions for a qualified franchise lawyer in each country. Laws, thresholds and exemptions change, and details depend on your structure. Do not rely on it to make legal decisions.

Expanding a franchise across borders means meeting a different set of disclosure rules in each country. Some countries have detailed franchise laws with fixed waiting periods; others rely on general contract law and good-faith duties. The overview below is a starting map, not a checklist.

## What "disclosure" usually means

Where franchise disclosure laws exist, they typically require the franchisor to give a prospective franchisee a document before any agreement is signed or money is paid, covering things like:

- Who the franchisor is, its directors and its litigation and insolvency history.
- Fees: initial fee, royalties, marketing fund contributions and other recurring costs.
- What the franchisee must buy, and from whom.
- Territory, term, renewal, transfer and termination.
- Financial statements of the franchisor, and any earnings information provided.
- A copy of the franchise agreement itself.

The waiting period between disclosure and signing gives the franchisee time to take advice.

## Country overview

| Country | Franchise-specific law | Disclosure timing (typical) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States | Yes — FTC Franchise Rule (16 CFR Part 436) | At least 14 calendar days before signing or paying | Disclosure via a Franchise Disclosure Document (FDD) with 23 items. Several states also require registration or filing before offering franchises, including California, New York, Illinois, Maryland, Minnesota and Washington. |
| Canada | Provincial laws in Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Ontario and Prince Edward Island | At least 14 days before signing or paying | Other provinces rely on general law. Requirements and exemptions differ by province. |
| Australia | Yes — the Franchising Code of Conduct | At least 14 days before entering the agreement or paying | The code was remade with changes taking effect in 2025; check the current version for the documents required, such as the key facts sheet and information statement. |
| United Kingdom | No franchise-specific law | No statutory period | General contract, consumer, competition and misrepresentation law apply. Membership of the British Franchise Association involves following its code of ethics. |
| France | Yes — Loi Doubin (Commercial Code, article L330-3) | At least 20 days before signing | A pre-contractual information document (DIP) is required. |
| Spain | Yes — Retail Trade Act and Royal Decree 201/2010 | At least 20 days before signing or paying | Written pre-contract information is required. |
| Italy | Yes — Law 129/2004 | At least 30 days before signing | The full agreement and specified information must be provided. |
| Netherlands | Yes — Franchise Act (in force since 2021) | At least four weeks before signing (standstill period) | Includes rules on providing information and on consultation with franchisees. |
| Belgium | Yes — rules on pre-contractual information for commercial partnership agreements | At least one month before signing | Applies to commercial partnership agreements, including franchising. |
| Germany | No franchise-specific law | No statutory period | Courts impose pre-contractual information duties; EU competition rules apply as everywhere in the EU. |
| Brazil | Yes — Franchise Law 13,966/2019 | At least 10 days before signing or paying | A franchise offering circular (COF) is required. |
| Mexico | Yes — Industrial Property law | Before signing (a period of 30 business days is commonly cited) | Disclosure document requirements apply; check the current federal law. |
| South Africa | Yes — Consumer Protection Act regulations | At least 14 days before signing | Also provides a cooling-off right after signing in certain cases. |
| Malaysia | Yes — Franchise Act 1998 | Disclosure before signing (commonly 10 days) | Franchisors generally must register before offering or operating franchises. |

Many other countries — including China, Japan, Indonesia and several Gulf states — have their own rules, ranging from registration requirements to sector-specific disclosure. If a country isn't listed here, that doesn't mean it has no rules.

## Practical steps before you expand

1. **Map your target countries** and get local advice for each one before you advertise franchises there. Advertising itself can be regulated.
2. **Build a disclosure master file** — company history, fees, supplier rules, territory, financial statements — that local counsel can adapt to each country's format.
3. **Track the clock.** Record when each prospect received the disclosure document and when they signed or paid, with evidence. A missed waiting period can make an agreement voidable in some places.
4. **Be careful with earnings claims.** Several countries regulate any statement about how much a franchisee might earn. Provide figures only in the form local law allows, with their basis.
5. **Plan for translations.** Some countries require disclosure in an official language.
6. **Keep agreements and versions organised.** You'll need to know which version of the disclosure document and agreement each franchisee received.
