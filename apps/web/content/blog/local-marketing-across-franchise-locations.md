Local marketing is where most franchise networks either grow or quietly fragment. Headquarters wants one recognisable brand. Franchisees want to fill their own calendar this week. Both are right, and the job of a marketing system is to let both happen at once.

This article sets out a model that works for networks from five to five hundred locations, regardless of industry. It covers who owns which decisions, how to fund local activity, the assets you need, and the review loop that keeps campaigns on brand without slowing them down.

## Start with a clear split of ownership

Most friction comes from unclear ownership rather than bad intent. Write down, once, which decisions belong to headquarters and which belong to each location. A useful default:

| Decision | Headquarters | Location |
|---|---|---|
| Brand identity (logo, colours, type, tone) | Owns | Uses |
| National campaigns and offers | Owns | Opts in or runs as given |
| Local offers and events | Sets the rules and limits | Owns within the rules |
| Location page content (hours, team, photos) | Provides the template | Owns |
| Paid local advertising | Approves templates and budgets | Runs and reports |
| Reviews and replies | Sets the reply policy | Owns day to day |

The exact split depends on your agreement and your market. What matters is that it's written, shared and reflected in the tools: if a field is "owned by headquarters", a location should not be able to edit it, and if a field is "owned by the location", headquarters should not overwrite it silently.

## Fund it in the open

Many franchise agreements include a brand or marketing fund contribution, usually a percentage of revenue. Treat that money with the same transparency you expect from franchisees:

- Publish how the fund is spent each year — national media, creative, technology, local co-op support.
- If you offer **co-op funding** (headquarters matches part of a location's local spend), publish the rules: what qualifies, the match rate, the cap and the evidence needed.
- Keep local spend and fund spend separate in reporting, and report each currency separately rather than converting everything into one number.

Franchisees accept rules they can see. They resist rules they discover after the invoice arrives.

## Build a kit, not a pile of files

A shared drive full of logos is not a marketing kit. A kit is a small set of ready-to-use templates with clear editable areas:

1. **Offer templates** for the three or four promotions every location runs (first visit, referral, seasonal, reactivation), with the headline and imagery locked and the offer details editable within limits.
2. **Event templates** for local launches, open days and community events.
3. **Location page sections** — team, timetable or menu, local photos, directions — that sit inside the brand layout.
4. **Email and SMS templates** with locked footers, consent text and unsubscribe handling.
5. **Paid social and search templates** with approved copy variations and a place for the local address and offer.

For each template, mark every field as *locked*, *editable* or *editable with approval*. That one decision removes most review back-and-forth.

## Review by exception

If every local post needs headquarters approval, headquarters becomes a bottleneck and franchisees work around it. Instead:

- Let locations publish freely inside editable fields.
- Require approval only for the things that carry real risk: prices outside the allowed range, health or results claims, legal disclaimers, and anything that changes a locked field.
- Set a response time for approvals (for example, one business day) and escalate automatically when it's missed.
- Keep a visible history of who changed what, so problems can be fixed and rolled back rather than argued about.

## Make local data visible to everyone who needs it

Local marketing only improves when locations can see what worked. Give each location its own view of enquiries by source, bookings or orders from campaigns, and review volume — and give headquarters the same view across the network, with filters by region and owner group.

Two rules keep that data trustworthy:

- **Attribute enquiries to the location the customer chose**, not the one that happened to answer the phone.
- **Never add up money in different currencies** without saying how it was converted. A network with locations in Canada and the UK has two revenue totals, not one. See [Multi-currency reporting for international networks](/blog/multi-currency-reporting-for-international-networks).

## A 90-day rollout

If you're starting from scratch, a realistic sequence looks like this:

1. **Weeks 1–3:** write the ownership table, agree it with your franchise advisory council or a few experienced owners, and publish it.
2. **Weeks 3–6:** build the first four templates and the location page layout. Test them with three locations of different sizes.
3. **Weeks 6–9:** fix what the first locations found confusing, then roll out to everyone with a short live walkthrough.
4. **Weeks 9–12:** switch on reporting by location, publish the first fund report, and schedule a quarterly review of templates.
