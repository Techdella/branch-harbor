In a multi-location business, the most expensive enquiry is the one nobody answers. It usually isn't laziness: the message landed in a shared inbox, or went to a location that thought another one would handle it. Routing fixes this by making ownership explicit. This guide uses real estate as the main example, but the same steps work for studios, clinics, schools and stores.

## Step 1: List your enquiry sources

Write down every way an enquiry arrives:

- Website forms (general contact, a specific listing, a valuation request).
- Property portals or marketplaces.
- Phone calls and voicemail.
- Email to shared addresses.
- Social media messages.
- Walk-ins.

For each source, note what information you receive — especially the location or listing the customer was looking at.

## Step 2: Decide the routing rules

Rules should be simple enough to explain in one sentence each. A common order for real estate:

1. **Listing enquiries go to the listing agent.** If the listing has several agents, notify all of them.
2. **If the listing agent is unavailable**, route round-robin to available agents in the same office.
3. **If nobody claims it** within a set time, it goes to the office pool, which the office manager monitors.
4. **General enquiries** (no listing) go to the office the customer chose, then round-robin.

For studios or clinics, replace "listing agent" with "the location the customer chose" and route to that location's team.

## Step 3: Set response-time targets

Agree targets per source and make them visible:

| Source | Target first response |
|---|---|
| Website form during opening hours | Within 1 hour |
| Portal enquiry | Within 1 hour |
| Form outside opening hours | By 10:00 next working day |
| Voicemail | Same day |

Measure against these targets, not against a vague "as soon as possible".

## Step 4: Protect privacy

Only the people handling an enquiry should see the customer's details. Headquarters usually needs totals — volumes, response times, conversion — not names and phone numbers. Record marketing consent with its source and date, and don't add enquirers to newsletters without it.

## Step 5: Close the loop

Every enquiry should end in a recorded outcome: booked, viewing arranged, not interested, no response, spam. Without outcomes, you can't tell which sources work.

## Step 6: Report by source and location

Review monthly:

- Enquiries by source and location.
- Median first-response time against target.
- Conversion to the next step (viewing, trial class, appointment, order).
- Enquiries that reached the pool, and why.

Share each location's figures with that location. Share network totals with headquarters.

## Common mistakes

- **One shared inbox for everyone.** Everyone sees it; nobody owns it.
- **Routing by who answered first.** It rewards speed over fairness and creates conflict between agents or locations.
- **No route for out-of-hours enquiries.** They wait until someone notices.
- **No record of outcomes.** You can't improve what you can't see.
