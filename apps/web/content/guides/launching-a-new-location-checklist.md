Opening a location is a project with dozens of moving parts and a fixed date. This checklist assumes the lease is signed and the fit-out is under way, and counts down the last twelve weeks. Adjust the timing to your industry and country — permits and payment accounts can take longer in some places.

## 12–10 weeks before opening

1. **Confirm the legal entity and owner.** In a franchise, confirm which company operates the location, who signs, and who the merchant of record will be for payments.
2. **Apply for licences and permits** that apply to your industry and city — trading, food hygiene, alcohol, signage, music licensing, health and safety. Note each expiry date.
3. **Open the payment account** for the location or owner. Identity checks can take days or weeks.
4. **Register for taxes** that apply locally and decide how prices will be shown (tax-inclusive or not).
5. **Order equipment with long lead times**: point of sale hardware, kitchen displays, access control, signage.

## 9–7 weeks

1. **Create the location record** in your systems: name, legal name, address, time zone, currency, phone, email and planned hours. Use the same name and address format everywhere.
2. **Set up the schedule, menu, services or rooms** the location will sell, with local prices.
3. **Prepare the location web page**: address, map, hours, team, photos (use temporary photos until the space is finished), and what customers can book or buy.
4. **Claim or create the local map listing** (such as a Google Business Profile) with the exact same name, address and phone number as the website.
5. **Plan the opening offer** within your brand's rules, and get approval if it needs it.

## 6–4 weeks

1. **Hire and invite staff**, giving each person access only to this location and only the role they need.
2. **Train the team** on bookings or orders, refunds, opening and closing procedures, and how enquiries reach them.
3. **Test payments end to end**: a real purchase, a refund and a payout to the right account.
4. **Start taking pre-opening enquiries or bookings** on the website, clearly showing the opening date.
5. **Brief local partners and neighbours** — community groups, nearby businesses, local press.

## 3–1 weeks

1. **Publish final photos and hours** once the space is finished.
2. **Run a soft opening** for friends, family or invited guests, and fix what goes wrong.
3. **Check every channel shows the same facts**: website, map listing, social profiles and booking pages.
4. **Set up reporting** so the location manager and headquarters can see enquiries, bookings or orders, and revenue in the location's currency from day one.
5. **Prepare an opening-week rota** with extra cover.

## Opening week

- Watch enquiries daily and reply within the same day.
- Ask happy first customers to leave a review, following platform rules.
- Hold a short daily debrief: what went wrong, what to change tomorrow.
- Change the location status from "opening soon" to "operating" everywhere.

## After 30 days

- Review the first month's numbers against the plan.
- Replace any remaining temporary content.
- Collect staff feedback on systems and training, and fix the checklist for the next opening.
