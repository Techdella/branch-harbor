Changing prices across a network sounds simple — update a number — but it touches websites, tills, menus, printed materials, subscriptions and customer trust in every location at once. This guide covers a safe sequence.

## 1. Decide what changes and where

Start with a table: product or service, current price per location (or price list), new price, effective date. Be explicit about:

- **Which locations follow the central price** and which have local pricing rights under their agreement.
- **Currencies.** Set a new price in each currency rather than converting at today's exchange rate; round to price points that look natural locally.
- **Tax display.** In many countries consumer prices must include taxes such as VAT or GST; in others, tax is added at checkout. Keep each market's rule.

In a franchise, check what your agreement allows. In many countries a franchisor can recommend resale prices but cannot require franchisees to sell at a fixed or minimum price; competition law varies by country, so take local advice.

## 2. Check the knock-on effects

Before publishing, list everything that depends on the old price:

- Online store, ordering and booking pages.
- Point-of-sale systems and printed menus or shelf labels.
- Active promotions and coupons — do they still make sense?
- Subscriptions and memberships — do existing customers keep their price, and for how long?
- Gift cards and prepaid packs — honour what customers already paid for.
- Marketplace and delivery-app listings.

## 3. Communicate before it happens

- **Tell location teams first**, with the reasons and an answer sheet for customer questions.
- **Tell existing subscribers in advance**, with the effective date and their options, as consumer law in many countries requires.
- **Update customer-facing pages on the effective date**, not before, unless you're announcing the change.

## 4. Publish in a controlled way

1. Prepare the change as a draft for all affected locations.
2. Review a summary: how many prices change, by location and currency, and which local overrides will be kept.
3. Publish at a set time — ideally outside opening hours.
4. Check a sample of locations on every channel within the first hour.

## 5. Handle exceptions

Some locations will need a different price: a high-rent site, a new market, a local competitor. Record each exception with its reason and an end date, so exceptions don't quietly become permanent.

## 6. Be ready to roll back

If a price is wrong, you need to restore the previous version everywhere quickly. Keep the previous price list and a record of who published what and when. A partial publish — some locations updated, some not — should be visible immediately, not discovered by customers.

## 7. Review after 30 days

Compare sales volume, average order value and margin per location, in each location's own currency. Look at exceptions and decide whether to keep them.
